# নদী — a real-time 3D Bengali village round a small river, at golden hour

Standalone site: Vite 8 + TypeScript 7 + three r186. Almost everything (terrain, trees, grass, houses, boats, most textures, sound) is generated in code. Two exceptions: six CC0 Poly Haven textures (colour + normal, 1K WebP, ≈4.5 MB) in `public/textures/` for the ground layers and the bark (chosen by the user in the second session), and the people, built as game characters are from MakeHuman's CC0 body, skeleton, skins, eyes, eyebrows, eyelashes and hair (`public/humans/`, ≈1.3 MB, made by `scripts/humans/`). There are no audio files and no map data.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` serves on http://localhost:5173 (on `localhost` only: pass `--base http://localhost:5173/` to the headless scripts, whose default is 127.0.0.1). `npm run build` writes `dist/` (hashed files in `dist/bundle/`). `npm run typecheck` runs `tsc --noEmit`. `npm run preview` serves `dist/`.
- Visual check: with the dev server running, `node scripts/headless/shoot.mjs <outDir> --base http://localhost:5173/ --shots '<json>'`. It saves PNGs and prints draw calls, triangles, frame ms and every page console message. It needs Chrome (set `CHROME_PATH` if Chrome is not at the default Windows path). The shot format and the debug API are documented in `HANDOFF.md`.
- `?quality=low|high` holds the quality tier (without it, `main.ts` drops to `low` by itself when even the adaptive resolution's floor runs under 30 fps).
- People (`scripts/humans/`, all MakeHuman, CC0): `node scripts/humans/fetch.mjs` downloads the base mesh, the default skeleton and weights and the macro targets into `scripts/humans/cache/` (git-ignored), and from the 280 MB system asset pack only the members `config.mjs` lists (`PACK_FOLDERS`), by range requests (`scripts/geo/lib/zip.mjs`). `node scripts/humans/build.mjs` builds the characters of `config.mjs` (`CHARACTERS`: `majhi`, `meye`; macro sliders, height, skins, eyes, brows, lashes, hair, `wear`) into `public/humans/humans.json` + `humans.bin` (deflate-raw; arrays shared between characters are stored once) and writes `cache/textures.json`; `python scripts/humans/textures.py` (Python 3 + Pillow) makes the WebP textures. Never hand-edit `public/humans/`.
- The build warns that a chunk is over 500 kB. That is expected: three.js is a large share of it.

## Invariants and gotchas (each one cost a bug)

- Never toggle `visible` on a light. Changing the light count recompiles every material (a 60 ms+ hitch at sunset). Set the intensity to 0 instead.
- Shadow maps use `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`. Redraw only when `Environment.aimShadow()` returns true; it checks whether the focus moved or the key light turned.
- Post passes sanitize NaN/Inf with comparisons, not `isnan()`. ANGLE/D3D may optimise `isnan()` away. A single bad pixel otherwise smears into black squares through the bloom chain. Birds once produced such a pixel, and the root cause is still unknown (see `HANDOFF.md`).
- Alpha-tested cards lose thin strokes in their mipmaps. Keep a dense core in the texture, as the kash plume does.
- The kash's plumes are hidden out of season by brightness alone (`seasonal: 'kash'` in `patch.ts`): the plume must stay the only bright part of `kashTexture`, and the test runs before the season's tint.
- Foliage cards use `foliage: true`, which keeps the vertex normal on back faces, so a crown shades as one soft volume.
- The water discards fragments inside the hero hull using `hullHalfBeamAtWater`, which is generated from `HERO_HULL`. Edit the hull only in `hull.ts`, and the GLSL follows.
- Fog is split between passes. The mirror pass fogs only water → object (the `uReflPass` branch of `applyAtmosphere`), and the water shader fogs camera → water.
- `tsconfig` has `erasableSyntaxOnly`: no constructor parameter properties, enums or namespaces. It also has `noUnusedLocals`/`noUnusedParameters`.
- Watch for aliasing of shared temporary vectors. `waterwayAt`, `Waterway.frame` and `journeyFrame` return reused objects (each its own): copy what you need before calling the same one again.
- A module that `geography.ts` imports must not import `geography.ts` back (it would read its constants before they exist): `roads.ts` takes the ground as a parameter of `buildRoads`, and the crossings' frames live in `sites.ts`, the river's levee and char in `waterways.ts`.
- Cameras and the film follow `boat.course` (smoothed); `boat.heading` wags about a degree with each stroke and would shake them.
- The hero group's rotation is `(rotX, heading, rotZ, 'YXZ')`: rotX positive is bow down, rotZ positive is starboard up. Local hull height is `heave + x·rotZ − z·rotX`.
- Boat and boatman form a loop: `hero.crew` (where his weight is) feeds `rig.update` one frame late; `hero.update` reads the body to pose him.
- The boatman's hands are placed on the paddle before his arms are solved (`Human.holdBar` uses last frame's elbow), and the arm IK then targets the wrist that leaves; `setHand` before `setArm`. Don't solve the arm first: the hand would float off the paddle.
- A `Human`'s bones are flat (all children of its `group`, no rotation in the bind); its meshes are skinned by dual quaternions (`DQ_BASE` etc. in `human.ts`), so every material on a `Human` goes through `dualQuaternions`, and nothing moves until `commit()`. Its meshes' bounding sphere is carried with the hips in `setTrunk`.
- The IK must use the body's own lengths (`body.dims`). Things laid on a person are bind points carried by a bone (`onChest`, `onHead`, a child of `bone(name)`).
- A fitted garment is a shell over the skin built in `scripts/humans/build.mjs` (edge from a smooth field, cut by `alphaTest`); loose cloth is sized from the `sections` the build measures. `ClothTube.update` lays each ring's depth on once, as given.
- The paddle stroke lives only in `paddle.ts`: change the stroke there and everything stays in step. `MEAN_THRUST` must match the drag at `CRUISE`.
- The journey is its own line (`journeyAt(s)`, `s` in metres along it), not a waterway's middle: the boat steers along it, the film and the stills place her by a share of `JOURNEY.length`, and what stands by the water is placed from the waterways (`Waterway.beside`, `bankAt`), never from the journey.
- A waterway's bank must be out of the water within a step of its edge (`wayGround`'s profile from `EDGE_DEPTH`): ground below the water's level beyond the marked water stood as dark, undrained hollows (the char's beach did, with hyacinth in them).
- Placements (houses, haystacks, trees, kash) must check `nearBackwater` (through `onDryLand` in vegetation), `onRoad` and `onLandmark`. Water shows only where `levelTexture` (water.ts) puts it: a new carved water must answer `inlandWaterAt`/`waterLevelAt` (or be painted through `eachWaterwayPoint`).
- Grass, flowers and crop patches ask `onRoad` for each plant: the ground lattice is too coarse to see a 1.2 m footpath. The terrain's plots keep off the ways and the banks' strip (`bankStrip`); the meadow keeps off the ghats.
- The paddy plots have a CPU twin of their shader layout (`glsl.ts` `plotWarp`/`plotFrame`/`PLOT_SIZE`/`PLOT_ANGLE` ↔ `crops.ts` `toPlots`/`fromPlots`/`PLOT`): change both together. A plot's crop comes from `plotRank`, in GLSL only (the terrain and the crops' vertex shader both read it); a per-plot random rank turned the fields into a checkerboard, and the old bands of turned grids drew seams across them.
- Floating plants keep to the slack water along the banks (`EDGE` in `drift.ts`) and the bil's lilies off the boat's way (`routeGap`).
- Seen from above the water, the haze ends at its surface (`applyAtmosphere` cuts the ray there).
- Past the world's edges the land fades into the haze (`EDGE_HAZE`, from `uLevelMap`); the free camera stays `EDGE` m inside them and below `CEILING`.
- Wave slopes evaluated per pixel must fade by the pixel's footprint (`waveSlopeLod` with `fwidth`).
- Lines drawn with a fixed colour floor glow against dark ground: light anything a pixel wide from the key light and the sky, and blend it by the share of the pixel it would really cover.
- A detail painted into a tiling texture repeats with it: the plaster texture's fallen patches stood in rows along the bridge's walls as black marks every 3.4 m (the user saw them). One-off marks (fallen plaster, stains) are placed as geometry; every plaster is `plasterTexture(seed, { fallen: false })`.
- `taperedTube` in `render/builder.ts` winds its triangles inward (known, not yet fixed project-wide). New single-sided geometry should use outward winding; the sanko's bamboo uses a double-sided material.
- Cloth vertex colours carry shading, so cloth materials need `vertexColors: true`; anything added with them must have a colour attribute (build it with `MeshBuilder`), or it renders black.
- Waves that move things must come from `waves.ts` (`waveHeight`/`waveSlope`, `hullWaves` for hulls, and the GLSL twins in shaders), so floating things ride what the eye sees.
- A cut in the film takes two frames: the director holds one frame (`Pipeline` `hold`) and opens the next scene on the following frame, which dissolves from the held one. The held texture must be unbound while it is being written. Keep this style and the scenes' camera angles: the user rejected a version that flew the camera between scenes and recomposed the shots.
- Anything streamed round the camera fades in (a band of distance, or growing from `aBorn`): a chunk that appears at once is the pop-in the user saw. Measure reach with `groundReach` so it also gives way when the camera is high.
- Vegetation's cost is draw calls (species shapes × slabs × mirror) and alpha-tested overdraw as much as triangles: before adding a species, shape or density, check `__river.stats('vegetation')` and the frame time.
- Debug-only code stays behind `?debug`. `shot()` freezes the frame loop: call `resume()` before holding keys. A probe's `import('/src/…')` gets its own module instances: their functions are right (the ground, the water, the waterways can be sampled that way) but `G` there is not the page's.
- All the water is inland: the river, the khals and the bil at `WATER_LEVEL`, the ponds at `POND_LEVEL` (`waterLevelAt`, −∞ on dry ground; `waterSurface(xz)` in a shader). Never compare a height with `uWaterLevel` alone.
- Anything the wind moves takes it from `windAt`/`gustAt` (CPU) or `windGust` (GLSL).
- Things that move must not cast shadows (the shadow map is not redrawn every frame): the hero boat.
- Camera-facing quads built in a vertex shader (the rain) must be `DoubleSide`.
- Anything that reads `Cover.zone` must allow for its sign (paddy > 0): the meadow uses `1 − |zone|`.
- The water is see-through: every material that can sit below the water must go through `patchMaterial`, or it will show through the water untinted.
- The ground blend samples only the two strongest layers, with the layer index as a texture coordinate. Arrays indexed in loops took frames from 16.7 ms to 26–32 ms in headless Chrome (ANGLE/Direct3D).
- The meadow builds only a few chunks a frame; stills call `meadow.update(camera.position, true)` to build them all.
- The orbit camera always looks at the boat, so it cannot look up at a high moon; stills of a high moon need `shot({ free, face })`.
- Sky event times come from `Clock.events()`; compare events by `clock.watching(ev)`, not identity.
- Season, weather and moon picks are eased and held for `SESSION_MS`; read the shown day and moon, not the clock's, for anything drawn.
- The quality tier (`setQuality` in `main.ts`) only scales what is already there: cull distances (`setCullReach`), the mirror's resolution, the shadow map, the grass's reach. Anything new that is costly far off should register with `cullBeyond`.
- The water mesh is laid round the camera in its vertex shader; anything that floats on it must use `waves.ts` (and `wake.ts` near the hero) with the same shelter.
- Distance culling (`render/cull.ts`) owns `visible` on the meshes registered with `cullBeyond`, and their bounding spheres are taken once, at build: register only things that stay put.
- `applyAtmosphere` integrates the fog and mist as a difference of the ray's two ends' exponentials; the product form is 0 × ∞ = NaN in fp32 once the eye is a few hundred metres up.

## Conventions

- TypeScript strict. Keep modules small and focused, put the reason in comments, and match the surrounding style.
- Keep it procedural apart from the six CC0 textures in `public/textures/` and the people's MakeHuman (CC0) data in `public/humans/`; ask before adding any other asset. External requests: those textures (same origin) and the Noto Serif Bengali Google Font, with system fallbacks.
- UI copy and captions are in Bangla. The people are realistic but generic: no likeness of any real person.
- The village is designed in `plan.ts`: change the layout there (and check it top-down) rather than placing things ad hoc in the builders. Every visible element gets the same level of finish; before adding anything, ask whether it belongs in a Bengali village and whether it makes the river journey more beautiful.
