# নদী — a real-time 3D Bengal river at golden hour, down the Moheshkhali channel

Standalone site: Vite 8 + TypeScript 7 + three r186. Almost everything (terrain, trees, grass, huts, boats, most textures, sound) is generated in code. The one exception, chosen by the user in the second session: six CC0 Poly Haven textures (colour + normal, 1K WebP, ≈4.5 MB) in `public/textures/` for the ground layers and the bark. There are no model or audio files.

- `HANDOFF.md` is the saved record of the session that built this: the requests, decisions, what exists, verification done, bugs fixed, known issues and the backlog. Read it before changing anything.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` serves on http://localhost:5173. `npm run build` writes `dist/` (hashed files in `dist/bundle/`). `npm run typecheck` runs `tsc --noEmit`. `npm run preview` serves `dist/`.
- Visual check (only when asked): with the dev server running, `node scripts/headless/shoot.mjs <outDir> --shots '<json>'`. It saves PNGs and prints draw calls, triangles, frame ms and every page console message. It needs Chrome (set `CHROME_PATH` if Chrome is not at the default Windows path). The shot format and the debug API are documented in `HANDOFF.md`.
- Geo data (`GEOGRAPHY_PLAN.md`, branch `geo-foundation`; `scripts/geo/`):
  - `node scripts/geo/fetch.mjs [ids…] [--force]` downloads the open data into `scripts/geo/cache/` (≈320 MB, git-ignored). Sources and licences are in `scripts/geo/config.mjs`; each file's checksum and date go into `cache/manifest.json`. Some hosts need the User-Agent (`lib/http.mjs`); big COGs are read in strips (`lib/raster.mjs`); tiles inside multi-GB zips are taken by range requests (`lib/zip.mjs`).
  - `node scripts/geo/tide-fit.mjs <years…>` fits the Cox's Bazar tide harmonics. The harmonics (speeds, V0, nodal terms, `predictor`) live in `src/geo/tide.ts`; `scripts/geo/lib/tide.mjs` re-exports it, so the bake and the scene predict the same tide. The bake copies the constants into `manifest.json` (`tide.constituents`).
  - `node scripts/geo/audit.mjs` prints the P0 audit and writes satellite crops to `cache/audit/`. The findings are in `GEO_AUDIT.md`.
  - `node scripts/geo/bake.mjs` writes `public/geo/`: terrain (dm, 30 m ground grid), kind, level (inland water's surface), cover, canopy, OSM lines, `channel.json` (the Moheshkhali channel's middle and half-widths), `buildings.bin` (OSM's footprints and Microsoft's where OSM has none: a ninth of the ordinary ones, every named or tagged one, at 1:3 places and real size, clear of each other; names in `buildings.json`), `places.json`, `manifest.json`. The vectors also carry the industrial sites' outlines (`site`: the power plant, its ash ponds, the port's works, substations) and point features (`nodes`: cranes, chimneys, wind turbines with their rating). The Matarbari plant's ash ponds (`ASH_PONDS`, dug after the DEMs) are baked in with their bunds: the north one (OSM way 1537055661) as inland water, the east one (way 1537056321, dry on Sentinel-2) as a bare bed. Inside `PORT_ZONE` (Matarbari port's works, reclaimed and dredged after the DEMs and JRC) land and water come from Sentinel-2's colour (green over red is water), a 3 × 3 majority twice over to drop stray cells, the reclaimed land at `PORT_ZONE.reclaimed`; the breakwaters stay water. Layers are deflate-raw (decompress with `DecompressionStream('deflate-raw')`). The world projection is `src/geo/project.ts` (the scripts import it; Node strips the types). Landmarks and what ground they must stand on are in `scripts/geo/places.mjs`.
  - The bake's kinds: water and flats joined to the sea, and from them the bare ground (WorldCover bare, water, wetland) below the spring tides' high water (a beach's lower sand), are the sea's (`tidal`); fields behind their bunds stay land.
  - `node scripts/geo/check.mjs` checks the bake against OSM's coastline and the landmarks, and draws OSM's lines over the bake and Sentinel-2 in `cache/view/`.
- `?quality=low|high` holds the quality tier (without it, `main.ts` drops to `low` by itself when even the adaptive resolution's floor runs under 30 fps). `?debug&geo` opens the GIS reference mode (`ui/geoMode.ts`, loaded on demand: WGS84/UTM readout of the camera and the cursor, drawn against baked ground, the source of the ground, the tide, the nearest place; OSM's lines, the coastline and the buildings' footprints draped on the ground; a pin per place coloured by confidence); `__river.goto(lat, lon, height)` puts the free camera over a real place.
- The build warns that a chunk is over 500 kB. That is expected: three.js is a large share of it (≈1.05 MB in all, ≈325 kB gzipped, in two chunks loaded at start; the GIS mode is a small chunk of its own, loaded only with `?debug&geo`).

## Architecture (`src/`)

- `geo/project.ts`: the lat/lon ↔ world projection (`MAP_SCALE = 3`, origin at Gorakghata jetty's landward end). `geo/data.ts`: loads the baked map (`public/geo/`) with top-level await before anything is built, so geography stays synchronous: `heightAt`, `kindAt` (`KIND`: land, mangrove, tidal, sea, inland), `coverAt` (`COVER`), `canopyAt`, `inlandSurfaceAt`, `cellAt`/`cellCentre`, `LAYERS` (the raw grids), `CHANNEL_POINTS`, `PLACES`/`place(id)`, `LINES` (OSM, world metres; `k: 'site'` for the industrial sites' closed outlines), `NODES` (cranes, chimneys, wind turbines), `BUILDINGS` (the baked buildings as arrays: centre, length, width, yaw, `BUILDING` kind, storeys, important), `WORLD` (the baked extent). On failure it shows a Bangla error in `#err`.
- `core/globals.ts`: `G`, the uniforms every scene shader shares (time, pass camera position, sun, key light, glow, fog, mist, night, wind and gust phase, the sea's level (`uWaterLevel`, the tide) and the last high water (`uTideHigh`), which water stands where (`tLevel`/`uLevelMap`, set by `buildWater`), the mirror's level (`uMirrorLevel`), the current (`uFlowScale`, signed: + ebb, − flood), the season's `uSeasonA`/`uSeasonB`/`uCrops`, wet, rain, overcast). Materials hold references to these objects, so mutate `.value` and never replace an object. Layers: `LAYER_WATER = 1`, `LAYER_NO_REFLECT = 2`. Both are drawn by the main camera only; the mirror camera sees layer 0.
- `render/glsl.ts`: `GLSL_COMMON` (uniform declarations, hash/noise/fbm, `windGust`, `plotFrame`/`PLOT_SIZE`/`cropShares` (the paddy plots and what each holds now), `waterLevels`/`waterSurface` (which water stands over a point and its surface: the tide over the sea's cells, an inland water's own level, none on dry ground), `hazeColor`, `applyAtmosphere`). Include it exactly once per shader. Also `CROP_REACH` (how far the 3D crops stand; inside it the terrain paints the ground under them).
- `render/patch.ts`: `patchMaterial(material, options)` injects the shared atmosphere into built-in materials, plus optional wind sway, grime, translucency, terrain paddy plots, riverbed caustics, foliage normals, and `splat` (the photo ground layers), through `onBeforeCompile`; also `seasonal: 'leaf' | 'tree' | 'kash'` (the season's tint, spring blossom on a share of the trees, kash plumes only in autumn) and `vertex` hooks. The terrain's `aZone` is signed: paddy plots > 0, salt pans < 0 (the salt works' layout has a JS twin, `LAYOUT`/`blockKind` in `world/coast.ts`; the paddy plots' one is in `world/crops.ts`); still water standing on the ground mirrors the sky (`atmBrine`). It sets `material.defines` and `customProgramCacheKey`; any new option must change that key. Its fog step also calls `underwater()` (in `glsl.ts`): anything below the water level fades into `G.uWaterBody` with the length of water it is seen through.
- `render/surfaces.ts`: loads the photo textures (`GROUND_LAYERS` grass, dirt, mud, sand, pebbles as two `DataArrayTexture`s, colour and normal, plus each layer's mean colour; and the bark). A set that fails to load is `null`, and the scene falls back to its painted look.
- `render/pipeline.ts`: `Pipeline` runs, in order: mirror pass (half-res `PlanarReflection`, `uReflPass = 1`, `uCamPos` = mirror camera) → scene (HalfFloat, MSAA ×4) → bright pass → 5-level bloom → radial light shafts → composite (ACES, grade, vignette, grain, chromatic fringe, fades, letterbox) straight to the canvas.
- `render/reflection.ts`: Reflector-style mirror camera for the plane at `G.uMirrorLevel` (the level of the water nearest the eye, chosen each frame by `water.update`), with an oblique near plane.
- `render/builder.ts`: `MeshBuilder` (merges procedural parts into one geometry), `rgb()` (sRGB hex → linear triple), `taperedTube`, `card`, `slab` (a box with metre UVs, turned by a yaw), `strut` (a beam between two points: girders, braces, galleries). `render/instancing.ts`: `instanced()` splits placements into slabs of 600 m along z by 1300 m across x (`chunk`, `across`) so off-screen slabs are culled. `render/cull.ts`: `cullBeyond(root, far)` records each mesh's world bounding sphere once; `updateCulling(eye)` (each frame, before drawing) leaves meshes undrawn beyond `far` (× `setCullReach`, 0.7 in the low tier) (`main.ts`: salt works 1100 m, small landmarks 1500 m, vegetation 1900 m). `render/textures.ts`: every texture is painted on a canvas and turned into a `DataTexture`. Rows are flipped so v = 1 is the canvas top, and `bleed` fills transparent texels with the average colour.
- `world/terrain.ts`: `Terrain`, the ground as quadtree patches round the camera (64 × 64 quads each, leaves 128 m, split nearer than 1.5 sizes, skirts wound outward, a few ms of building a frame with the nearest built ancestor standing in, `update(eye, true)` builds all for stills); colour and layer weights per vertex from the map's cover (`LOOK` per `COVER` class, blended over the four cells round a point). It also exports `groundAt(x, z)` / `Cover`: painted colour, field zone (signed, see above) and the photo-layer weights at a point. The terrain's `aSplat` attribute and the meadow's grass density both come from it.
- `world/crops.ts`: 3D crops in the paddy plots round the camera (out to `CROP_REACH`): rice in rows (seedling, growing, ripe with nodding panicles, stubble) and winter mustard in flower, in three LODs (real plants < 20 m, row cards + canopy < 44 m, canopy only beyond). Each patch works out its plot's crop in the vertex shader from `plotFrame`/`cropShares`, so the year turning regrows the fields with nothing rebuilt; rice and mustard meshes share instance buffers (the absent one is collapsed below ground); the wind bends each plant from its foot (`sway`). Patches are laid per plot between its bunds.
- `world/meadow.ts`: dense blade grass, wild flowers, waterline pebbles and, on the dunes behind beaches open to the Bay (`nearOpenBeach`), beach morning glory (Ipomoea pes-caprae) with the grass thinned to tussocks, round the camera, built in 12 m chunks (two levels of detail, a few chunks per frame, from a 1.5 m height lattice) into one `InstancedMesh` per layer. `world/drift.ts`: water hyacinth as position-based dynamics: 720 plants (each its own body, tied to neighbours in mats by tension-only root links that snap when overstretched) and 160 leaves, dragged toward the local current and a little downwind, colliding through a spatial hash, pushed out of the hero hull and by the oar blade, nudged off the banks, bobbing and tilting on the shared waves; recycled round the viewer. It also meets `DriftObstacles` from `Fishing.obstacles()`: the anchored boats' hulls, the A-frames' poles, and the simulated nets (a wall where the netting crosses the surface; a rising net scoops up what floats over it and holds it until it goes back in). `world/ponds.ts`: lily pads and flowers on the village ponds and the small wetlands, and the wetlands' reeds and floating hyacinth.
- `world/geography.ts` is the single source of truth for land and water, over the baked map: `terrainHeight` (the map, plus the temple's terrace, the roads' embankments (`roadGround`), the bil's bowl and OSM's khals, streams and drains cut into dry ground), `waterLevelAt` (the sea's `G.uWaterLevel`, or an inland water's own level), `seaWaterAt` / `inlandWaterAt`, and the channel frame the scene was first built along, now answering for the real Moheshkhali channel from `geo/channel.json` (it runs north–south, every point north of the one before): `riverX`, `edgeHalfWidth` (side +1 west, the island's), `halfWidth`, `riverDepth`, `streamSpeed`, `downstream`, `bankInfo`, `riverLane` (= `boatLane`: off the island's shore), `CHANNEL_SPAN` (its ends; placements laid along the banks keep within it), `JOURNEY_Z0` → `JOURNEY_Z1` (north to south, 6800 → −1400). The world is the baked area (`WORLD_X0..X1`, `WORLD_Z1..Z0`, ≈10.3 × 15.5 km): x west, z north, heights real metres about MSL. ## Invariants and gotchas (each one cost a bug)

- Never toggle `visible` on a light. Changing the light count recompiles every material (a 60 ms+ hitch at sunset). Set the intensity to 0 instead.
- Shadow maps use `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`. Redraw only when `Environment.aimShadow()` returns true; it checks whether the focus moved or the key light turned.
- Post passes sanitize NaN/Inf with comparisons, not `isnan()`. ANGLE/D3D may optimise `isnan()` away. A single bad pixel otherwise smears into black squares through the bloom chain. Birds once produced such a pixel, and the root cause is still unknown (see `HANDOFF.md`).
- Alpha-tested cards lose thin strokes in their mipmaps. Keep a dense core in the texture, as the kash plume does.
- Foliage cards use `foliage: true`, which keeps the vertex normal on back faces, so a crown shades as one soft volume.
- The water discards fragments inside the hero hull using `hullHalfBeamAtWater`, which is generated from `HERO_HULL`. Edit the hull only in `hull.ts`, and the GLSL follows.
- Fog is split between passes. The mirror pass fogs only water → object (the `uReflPass` branch of `applyAtmosphere`), and the water shader fogs camera → water.
- `tsconfig` has `erasableSyntaxOnly`: no constructor parameter properties, enums or namespaces. It also has `noUnusedLocals`/`noUnusedParameters`.
- Watch for aliasing of shared temporary vectors. The boatman's arm IK broke because an output vector was also read as an input.
- Cameras and the film follow `boat.course` (smoothed); `boat.heading` wags about a degree with each stroke and would shake them.
- The hero group's rotation is `(rotX, heading, rotZ, 'YXZ')`: rotX positive is bow down, rotZ positive is starboard up. Local hull height is `heave + x·rotZ − z·rotX`.
- Boat and boatman form a loop: `hero.crew` (where his weight is) feeds `rig.update` one frame late; `hero.update` reads the body to pose him.
- The boatman's hands are placed on the paddle before his arms are solved (`placeHand` uses last frame's elbow), and the arm IK then targets the wrist that leaves. Don't solve the arm first: the hand would float off the paddle. `hero.ts` passes each hold as a point on the bar plus the bar's direction toward the thumb.
- The paddle stroke lives only in `paddle.ts`: the blade's path, the boatman's body, the boat's thrust and the water's rings all read it, so change the stroke there and everything stays in step.
- The boat's route must keep moving toward −z (steering looks a fixed distance ahead in z): the lane follows the real channel, which runs north–south. Keep the journey inside `CHANNEL_SPAN`; beyond its ends `riverX` holds the end point's x.
- Placements (huts, haystacks, trees) must check `nearBackwater` (through `onDryLand` in vegetation) or they stand in a khal or the bil, and `onRoad` or they stand on a road. The terrain's cover already keeps grass and paddy off the roads. Water shows only where `levelTexture` (water.ts) puts it: the sea's over sea, tidal and mangrove cells, inland water over inland cells and what it adds (the bil, and every khal through `eachWaterwayPoint`). A new carved water must be painted there too. Khals are narrower than the field's 4 m texels, so the inland water is discarded only where the field's bed is well above its level; the depth test hides it under the banks.
- `underwater()` tints what lies under the sea's water clearer and greener-blue toward the open Bay (by `seaExposure`) than the channel's silt.
- Seen from above the water, the haze ends at its surface (`applyAtmosphere` cuts the ray there): the height fog's density grows below sea level, and a deep bed (the dredged channel) used to come out brighter than a shallow one.
- Wave slopes evaluated per pixel must fade by the pixel's footprint (`waveSlopeLod` with `fwidth`): short waves sampled too coarsely alias into rings round the eye on open water. The grid's swell displacement fades the same way by its vertex spacing.
- Lines drawn with a fixed colour floor glow against dark ground (the power lines' wires did, at night and in shadow): light anything a pixel wide from the key light and the sky, and blend it by the share of the pixel it would really cover.
- `taperedTube` in `render/builder.ts` winds its triangles inward (known, not yet fixed project-wide; see `HANDOFF.md`). New single-sided geometry should use outward winding, as `anatomy.ts` does.
- Cloth vertex colours carry shading, so cloth materials need `vertexColors: true`; anything added with them must have a colour attribute (build it with `MeshBuilder`), or it renders black.
- Waves that move things must come from `waves.ts` (`waveHeight`/`waveSlope`, `hullWaves` for hulls, and the GLSL twins in shaders), so floating things ride what the eye sees. The sea state is set once a frame by `updateSea()` from the wind, gusts and rain (in `main.ts`'s `updateSky`); each wave's amplitude reaches the shaders as `G.uWaveA/B/C`. Hulls use `hullWaves` (the waves averaged over the waterline), not point samples: a few samples let short, steep chop roll the boat ±25°.
- A cut in the film takes two frames: the director's update runs before the frame is drawn, so it holds one frame (`Pipeline` `hold`) and opens the next scene on the following frame, which dissolves from the held one. The held texture must be unbound while it is being written (a feedback loop otherwise silently cancels the draw).
- Anything that floats on the sea's water outside the channel reads `waveHeight`/`hullWaves`, which include the swell and the open sea's higher wind waves by `exposureAt`; inland water and the channel's inner reaches have none. The water shader takes the same from `seaExposure` (the level texture's alpha) and the depth.
- Vegetation's cost is draw calls (species shapes × slabs × mirror) and alpha-tested overdraw as much as triangles: before adding a species, shape or density, check `__river.stats('vegetation')` (instances and triangles per shape) and the frame time in the hills and village views.
- Debug-only code stays behind `?debug`. The debug `shot()` places the boat after the water level has settled; `nets()`, `wetlands()`, `matsAt()`, `caught()` and `trace()` are there for checking the fishing, the hyacinth, the wetlands and the route (see `HANDOFF.md`); `walk(x, z, heading)`, `voyage()` and `go(id, how)` (the map's own actions) for walking and the voyages. `shot()` freezes the frame loop: call `resume()` before holding keys (dispatched `KeyboardEvent`s reach the real handlers). A long `trace()` leaves the page idle long enough to start the film: send a key straight after it. A probe's `import('/src/…')` gets its own module instances: their functions are right but `G` there is not the page's (read the shader clock as `__river.info().time`, the water through `seaAt`).
- The sea's surface is `G.uWaterLevel`: the tide, ≈3.6 m between spring low and high water (−2.3 to +2.7 m over a year), never y = 0; inland water stands at its own level (`waterLevelAt`, `BIL.level`, a pond's `level`, `BRIDGE.water`). Anything that floats, wades or is clamped above the water reads its water's level, on the CPU (`waterLevelAt`, −∞ on dry ground) or in a shader (`waterSurface(xz)`); never compare a height with `uWaterLevel` alone, or the fields behind their bunds go under water at high tide. Floating bodies with a heave spring move with the level's change directly (`BoatBody`, `Mooring`), so a jump of the tide (a film scene's hour) does not leave them in the air. Things standing on the bank clear the highest tide (`HIGH_WATER` in `fishing.ts`).
- Anything the wind moves takes it from `windAt`/`gustAt` (CPU) or `windGust` (GLSL), so trees, water, cloth, boats, nets and plants agree.
- Things that move must not cast shadows (the shadow map is not redrawn every frame, so their shadows would stay behind): the hero boat, the fishing boats, the levers, arms and nets.
- Camera-facing quads built in a vertex shader (the rain) must be `DoubleSide`: which face shows depends on the slant. The rain was invisible for a whole task because of this.
- `LiftNet` skips its cloth step for steps under 4 ms: the strings' corrections over a tiny step become huge forces on the lever.
- Anything that reads `Cover.zone` must allow for its sign (paddy > 0, salt pans < 0): the meadow uses `1 − |zone|`.
- Floating plants in the small wetlands are set only over beds below the driest season's water (−0.5 m), or they would float over dry ground.
- The water is see-through: every material that can sit below the water must go through `patchMaterial`, or it will show through the river untinted.
- The ground blend samples only the two strongest layers, with the layer index as a texture coordinate. Arrays indexed in loops (`w[i]`, `cs[i]`) took frames from 16.7 ms to 26–32 ms in headless Chrome (ANGLE/Direct3D).
- The meadow builds only a few chunks a frame; stills call `meadow.update(camera.position, true)` to build them all.
- The orbit camera always looks at the boat, so it cannot look up at a high moon; stills of a high moon need `shot({ free, face })`.
- `ClothTube.update` lays each ring's depth (`b`) on once, as given. An earlier note here said twice; the walker's fatua sank into his chest when he passed half. `girl.ts` passed half too and her kameez came out flat from the side; it passes the full depth now. Keep cloth off the body with a `collide` where limbs pass through it.
- The map's jumps (on foot, from above, a long voyage) act at the thickest of the mist, and only there does the camera snap (`rig.aboard(false)`, `walkFrom`); a short voyage from the boat sets off at once.
- The walker is hidden whenever the camera leaves him (`aboard`, `setMode`); anything that moves the boat elsewhere (`rig.place`, which the film's stage uses) brings him back aboard first, or he would be left standing where she was.
- Sky event times come from `Clock.events()`; compare events by `clock.watching(ev)`, not identity (the cache is rebuilt each day and on a moon change).
- Season, weather and moon picks are eased (the year turns over `YEAR_TURN_SECONDS` through `shownDay()`, the moon through `shownMoon()`), and each holds for `SESSION_MS` before `backToToday()`; read the shown day and moon, not the clock's, for anything drawn.
- Two layouts have CPU twins of a shader: the paddy plots (`glsl.ts` ↔ `crops.ts`) and the salt works (`patch.ts` ↔ `coast.ts` `LAYOUT`/`blockKind`). Change both together. So does the surf: `swellBreaking` in `waves.ts` has a GLSL and a CPU copy (the water's foam, the soundscape's breakers).
- What must stand right at the water's edge (the bank nets, the kash, the film's kash stand) finds the real shore with `bankAt(z, side, level)`: `edgeHalfWidth` is a smoothed low-water line, often tens of metres off the drawn bank.
- The quality tier (`setQuality` in `main.ts`) only scales what is already there: cull distances (`setCullReach`), the mirror's resolution (`Pipeline.setReflectionScale`), the shadow map (`Environment.setShadowMapSize`, which lets the old map go so a new size takes), the grass's reach (`Meadow.setReach`). Anything new that is costly far off should register with `cullBeyond` so the tier reaches it.
- The data's credits come from `manifest.json` `attribution` (written by `bake.mjs`); the help sheet lists them (`#sources`, `controls.ts`) and the README repeats them. A new data source must add its credit there. FABDEM is CC BY-NC-SA: the site stays non-commercial.
- The water mesh is laid round the camera in its vertex shader; anything that floats on it must use `waves.ts` (and `wake.ts` near the hero) with the same shelter, or it will sit off the surface.
- Distance culling (`render/cull.ts`) owns `visible` on the meshes registered with `cullBeyond`, and their bounding spheres are taken once, at build: register only things that stay put, and never set `visible` on those meshes yourself.
- Landmark positions come from `scripts/geo/places.mjs` (baked to `places.json`, with sources); `geography.ts` derives `GORAKGHATA`, `ADINATH`, `COXS_BAZAR`, `SHUTKI_YARDS` from them and `moheshkhali.ts` builds from those; huts, trees, grass and salt pans keep off them through `onLandmark` (trees also through `onTempleHill`, which keeps the temple visible from the channel). Move a landmark there and its clearing follows.
- Salt pans are ground (their brine is painted by the terrain shader), not inland water: the bake turns inland cells under salt-pan cover into land. Inland water's level is the median of the DEM's surface over the water round it (±2 cells), and water the DEM puts on a slope is ground (`INLAND_SLOPE`); otherwise ghers become tilted sheets.
- The map is baked, not drawn: change ground, water or landmarks in `scripts/geo/` and rerun `bake.mjs` (then `check.mjs`); never hand-edit `public/geo/`.
- `applyAtmosphere` integrates the fog and mist as a difference of the ray's two ends' exponentials. The product form (`exp(-y0 f) · (1 − exp(−dist k)) / k`) is 0 × ∞ = NaN in fp32 once the eye is a few hundred metres up, and the whole frame goes dark.

## Conventions

- TypeScript strict. Keep modules small and focused, put the reason in comments, and match the surrounding style.
- Keep it procedural apart from the six CC0 textures in `public/textures/`; ask before adding any other asset. External requests: those textures (same origin) and the Noto Serif Bengali Google Font, with system fallbacks.
- UI copy and captions are in Bangla. No realistic faces or likenesses of real people: the boatmen are faceless and stylised.
